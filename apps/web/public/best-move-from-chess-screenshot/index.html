You have a picture of a chess position and one question: what is the best move? Paste the picture. fenshot reads the board in about a second, and one click opens the position with a free Stockfish engine that shows the best move.

From a screenshot to the best move

  1. Take a screenshot of the position, or copy the image.
  2. Paste it on fenshot.com (Ctrl+V works anywhere on the page) or drop the file in.
  3. Look at the board fenshot shows and set the side to move.
  4. Press Analyze on Kibitzo. The engine starts by itself and draws the best move on the board as an arrow.

You can also press Analyze on Lichess and switch the engine on there. Both are free and need no account.

Whose move is it?

A picture does not say whose turn it is, and the best move for White is not the best move for Black. That is why fenshot asks for the side to move before you analyze. It does notice when a screenshot was taken from Black's side of the board and turns the position the right way round.

An engine needs the exact position

One piece on the wrong square and the best move is a different move. So fenshot shows you the board it read before anything else, warns you when it is not sure about a square, and lets you put it right on the spot: move a piece, take one off, add one.

Which pictures work?

Screenshots from chess.com and Lichess in any theme, frames from YouTube videos and streams, diagrams from books and PDFs, puzzles posted on Reddit or sent in a chat. Boards drawn flat, seen from above, work best.

For study, not for a game in progress

Use it on finished games, puzzles, books and videos. Asking an engine for a move in a game you are still playing is against the rules of every chess site.

It stays on your machine

The recognition model runs in your browser via WebAssembly. The image is never uploaded, which is why it works offline once loaded and why there is no account, no quota and no cost.
